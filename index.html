Built density vs gross density

Gross density divides homes by a neighbourhood's whole area, so a neighbourhood that has just started building looks sparse no matter how dense its buildings are. Built density divides homes by only the land that has finished housing on it, so neighbourhoods at different stages can be compared fairly. How these are worked out.

What land produces in municipal tax

Municipal property tax is estimated as each property's assessed value times the City's municipal tax rate for its class. Education tax is left out. It is shown per hectare of land with finished housing, and per home. This shows what land produces in tax, not what it costs the City to serve; servicing costs are not published by neighbourhood. Permits and housing starts

What is the difference between a building permit and a housing start?

Most tabs count homes on City of Edmonton building permits. The Housing starts tab uses CMHC housing starts. They describe the same building activity at different stages, with different rules.

City of Edmonton building permitCMHC housing start
What it isApproval from the City to build. Issued before construction begins.Confirmation that construction has begun: usually when concrete is poured for the whole footing around the building.
Who records itThe City's permit system, when the permit is issued.CMHC's Starts and Completions Survey, through site visits by enumerators.
TimingCounted on the issue date. A building appears here first, and some permits are never built or are re-issued.Counted in the month construction starts, often months after the permit. A large building is a single start for all its units.
What counts as a unitThe change in dwelling units on the permit, including secondary suites, garden suites and office-to-residential conversions.New self-contained units on new permanent foundations. Renovations and alterations inside existing buildings are excluded, so suites added to existing homes and conversions are not starts. Nursing homes, hotels and lodging houses are excluded too.
Housing typesThe City's building-type labels. Stacked and "multi-unit" townhouses are often filed as row houses.Single, semi-detached, row (3 or more side-by-side units), and "apartment and other", which includes stacked townhouses, duplexes and triplexes.
TenureNot recorded.Intended market: homeowner, rental, condo or co-op. "Rental" means built to be rented, regardless of who finances it (purpose-built rental).

Why don't the permit and start numbers match?

Use permits to see where, and what, the City is approving. Use starts to see what construction actually began and who it is being built for. Over several years the two move together; in a single year, timing and definitions keep them apart.

What counts as a purpose-built rental?

CMHC records the intended market of every start: homeowner, rental, condo or co-op. "Rental" means the home was built to be rented, regardless of who finances it. A condo unit that an owner later rents out is still counted as condo. Building permits don't record tenure, so purpose-built rental figures are only on the Housing starts tab.

Density and tax base

What is the difference between built density and gross density?

Gross density is homes divided by the whole area of a neighbourhood, including roads, parks, schools and land not yet built on. Built density is homes divided by only the residential land that has finished buildings on it. A new neighbourhood that is half built looks sparse on gross density even if its buildings are dense; built density lets it be compared with a mature neighbourhood.

How are homes counted for density?

Homes in finished buildings: 2021 census dwellings plus homes on later building permits, leaving out permits from the last three years that have no occupancy date yet.

Which land counts as residential?

Lot sizes come from the City's property assessment roll. Condo units each carry their share of the parcel, so they add up correctly. Land with no finished building is left out, and large unsubdivided parcels (over 2 hectares with no registered plan) are kept separate. Roads, parks and schools are not on the roll.

How is municipal tax estimated?

Each property's assessed value times the City's municipal tax rate for its class. Education tax is left out. These are estimates, not actual tax bills.

Why isn't non-residential tax shown?

The City's open data includes tax-exempt properties such as post-secondary campuses, hospitals and City-owned land without marking them, so tax can't be estimated reliably for non-residential property. The Tax base tab shows assessed value per hectare instead, which is not tax.

What are "typical" house and condo values?

Medians of assessed value for the roll year. A house is a residential account on its own lot, with a finished building. A condo is a condo unit worth at least $75,000, which leaves out parking stalls and storage units. A value is shown only where there are at least 10 properties.

Updates

How often is the site updated?

Every Monday the site automatically pulls the latest City of Edmonton open data and CMHC figures. CMHC publishes its figures monthly. If CMHC can't be reached, the previous week's figures stay in place until the next update.
